Lyriel combat and projectile pooling

I implemented Lyriel’s basic attack, charged attack and arrow volley, together with a projectile pool that reuses arrows during combat.

Each launched arrow receives its direction, speed, lifetime, target and damage. It checks target validity and applies damage on impact before returning to the pool. Shared character and ability classes handle common behavior such as cooldowns and attack locking, while Lyriel’s character script provides access to the pool and spawn point.

I later extended the pooling workflow with prewarming during level initialization. Projectile visual variants, charge glow and hit effects are prepared and reused, and frequently accessed components are cached. Target acquisition uses the shared gameplay target registry. These changes addressed first-use and recurring spikes from creating and destroying visuals during attacks.

Player targeting controller

I built directional target selection for both players, with independent indicators and rules that account for combat priorities and ability input.

The controller filters candidates by aim cone and range, then scores their alignment and distance. A switch margin and cooldown prevent rapid oscillation between similarly scored targets. Ability states determine when targeting can use the aim input, keeping selection compatible with attacks that also use the right stick.

Enemies take priority when available. Ordinary breakables are excluded while enemies are in range, while healing and explosive objects remain valid because they affect combat. Later refinements improved input thresholds, score normalization and switching when a target leaves the aim cone.

I also replaced repeated scene searches with a shared registry. Enemies and breakables register and leave through their lifecycle, so newly spawned enemies become available without rescanning the scene.

Arthur miniboss attacks

My Arthur work covered Heavy Swipe, Side Sweep, Charging Slam and Earth Hammer, including attack states, phase variations, damage areas and animation timing.

I centralized repeated cone, radius and area calculations in ArthurAttackExecutor. ArthurAttackConfig exposes the attack parameters in the Inspector, and debug drawing displays the damage zones so their size, direction and timing can be checked against the animations. I also implemented the player stun behavior needed by the relevant attacks.

As the boss model and animation clips changed, I adapted the attacks, split Side Sweep into left and right states, and adjusted Charging Slam playback to match movement and impact. Later tuning refined attack speeds and recovery windows.

The boss was developed collaboratively. Another gameplay programmer handled its controller, aggro and movement; my responsibility was attack execution, configuration and tuning within that framework.

Gameplay events and combat room progression

I created a reusable event framework and used it to control cooperative combat-room progression.

GameplayEventTrigger detects when both players enter an assigned area and starts a GameplayEventAction. The trigger can run once, allowing encounters, tutorials and camera sequences to share an activation mechanism while keeping their behavior separate.

CombatAreaEvent closes navigation blockers at the entrance and exit when the encounter starts. It tracks the enemies assigned to that room and removes the blockers after they are all defeated. Enemy and blocker references are configured in the Inspector, so the same script supports rooms with different layouts and enemy groups.

Cooperative tutorials and objectives

I implemented configurable tutorials that close when both players confirm or when a player completes the requested gameplay action.

PopUpEvent defines images, transitions, timings, close mode and whether gameplay input is locked. PopUpController manages the active popup and progression through multiple slides. Requiring confirmation from both players prevents one player from closing an instruction before the other is ready.

ObjectiveEvent supports movement, basic attack, charged attack and ability objectives, assigned to a particular player. These instructions can remain visible during gameplay and close when the action is performed. Both popup types use the shared gameplay event framework, so the team can place and configure tutorials in the level.

Cinematic camera transitions

I built camera events that introduce encounters while coordinating camera movement, gameplay control and timed object actions.

CameraTransitionEvent and CameraTransitionController move the camera through authored points, hold a view and blend back to gameplay. The system supports smooth paths and field-of-view changes. During a sequence, input can be disabled, players protected from damage and the HUD faded.

Each CameraTransitionStep defines movement and hold durations, movement mode and optional field of view. Actions at step start, arrival or completion can move objects to a point, follow a path or play an animation with a configurable delay. A Hold While Triggered mode supports camera zones that stay active while occupied.

I used this workflow for a paladin entrance and war cry, and a banquet-room sequence in which five enemies move into starting positions. The team can tune the sequence directly through the editor.

Death taunt and forced enemy movement

I reworked Death’s existing taunt into a phased pull ability and implemented reusable forced enemy movement.

On release, the ability stores the cast position and aim direction, then checks enemies inside the cone after an impact delay. Affected enemies move toward a configurable stopping distance from the original cast position while staying constrained to the navigation mesh. Enemies already inside that distance are not pushed backward.

The pull temporarily takes control of enemy movement and coordinates with existing behavior: it cancels the Paladin’s charge and Archer’s somersault, pauses Skeleton movement during attacks and prevents Summoner teleporting. Arthur remains immune during Charging Slam. Damage timing accounts for whether an enemy needs to be pulled, and Death regains movement when the pull starts.

Shadow Execution and Reaper Gauge feedback

I extended the existing cooperative execution mechanic with configurable damage rules, enemy health-bar previews and stronger full-gauge feedback.

Shadow Execution uses the higher of a fixed damage value and a percentage of enemy maximum health, with configurable execution thresholds. I introduced ShadowExecutionConfig and adjusted contribution calculations for the number of gauge segments.

When execution is ready, enemy health bars preview the expected remaining health and visually distinguish execution eligibility. The preview fades in, follows health changes and clears when the ability is consumed or unavailable. An impact pop reinforces the execution.

I also enhanced the full Reaper Gauge with an initial scale pop, breathing glow, sequential energy surges and a frame echo. A blue-white screen grade reinforces readiness. To support this, I extended the existing LUT effect with imported .cube assets, adjustable strength, shader blending and editor controls.

Gameplay leadership and production

I led regular gameplay programming meetings in which each programmer explained current work and next steps. Shared context helped coordinate related features and made integration needs visible. I maintained gameplay planning information, including task ownership, priorities and review status.

I regularly built and tested teammates’ branches, reviewed pull requests and documented gameplay issues with screenshots and videos. This included checking attack behavior, targeting, movement and risks to scene serialization.

I supported production throughout the project and became the main producer during its final four months. I followed up with team members, planned around availability, prioritized milestone work and helped the team decide which parts of the planned scope to cut when necessary.
