01 / RANGED COMBAT

Reuse the projectile.
Keep the impact.

I implemented Lyriel’s basic, charged and volley attacks, and a pool that acquires, launches and returns projectile instances.

Each arrow receives its direction, speed, lifetime, target and damage. Damage is applied at impact, keeping visual travel and hit timing connected. Common ability behavior handles cooldowns and attack locking.

Later revisions prewarmed projectiles and reused their visual variants, charge glow and hit effects. I cached frequently accessed components and reused the shared target registry to reduce repeated scene searches.

02 / PLAYER TARGETING

Aim with intent.

I built directional target selection for both players, with separate visual indicators and configurable selection rules.

Candidates are filtered by range and aim cone, then scored using alignment and distance. A switch margin and cooldown help prevent oscillation between similar targets. Ability states control when targeting can consume input.

A shared registry tracks enemies and breakables throughout their lifecycle, making newly spawned targets available without repeatedly scanning the scene.

03 / MINIBOSS ATTACKS

Make the danger readable.

My Arthur work covered Heavy Swipe, Side Sweep, Charging Slam and Earth Hammer, including attack states, hit detection and timing.

I centralized cone, radius and area calculations in ArthurAttackExecutor and exposed parameters through ArthurAttackConfig. Debug drawing helped align damage areas with animations and tune the players’ opportunities to dodge.

This was shared work: Mikhail developed Arthur’s boss controller, aggro and movement. My responsibility was the attack implementation and its configuration tools.

04 / LEVEL SCRIPTING

One event framework.
Different encounters.

I created a shared trigger-and-action structure for combat rooms, cooperative tutorials and cinematic camera sequences.

Combat rooms close their navigation blockers when both players enter and reopen after the assigned enemies are defeated. Tutorials support confirmation from both players or completion of a gameplay objective. Camera sequences can lock input, fade the HUD and coordinate timed object actions.

I also reworked Death’s taunt and contributed to Shadow Execution and Reaper Gauge feedback, using visible previews to communicate effects during combat.

05 / WORKING WITH THE TEAM

Share the context.

I led gameplay programming meetings where each programmer explained current work and next steps. I reviewed pull requests and tested branches to check behavior in game, and supported task planning and follow-ups as producer during the project’s final four months.
